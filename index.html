The Concept

Ballet meets Metal

One of the main inspirations for this collection was the Sexy Robot exhibition by Hajime Sorayama in Shanghai, which I visited in 2025. The futuristic aesthetic, cold reflective surfaces, and combination of the human body with industrial elements inspired me to explore the contrast between external fragility and inner strength

While researching the topic in the library, I became particularly interested in two books that helped shape the concept of the collection. The first was Masafumi Monden’s study of ballet aesthetics and femininity in Japanese shōjo manga, the second was Michel Foucault’s *Discipline and Punish*

Lina Nguen
Inspiration
The title *Whisper of Motion* reflects the quiet tension within movement.

the idea that ballet is not only about visible grace, but also about the unseen effort, discipline, and emotional intensity present in every gesture. It expresses the contrast between softness and strength, stillness and motion, which lies at the core of the collection.

Interview

A Conversation with a Ballerina

01

What emotions did you feel when you first stepped onto the stage?

"At first, there was overwhelming nervousness, almost fear — but then it quickly turned into euphoria."

02

What was the most difficult thing about practicing ballet?

"Overcoming physical pain and mental pressure. Every practice had to be perfect, no mistakes allowed. But when you stand on stage, everything is worth it."

03

How did ballet change you as a person?

"It taught me discipline and perseverance. Even when your body hurts, you still have to smile and soar. That is quiet strength."

04

If you had the chance, would you return to ballet?

"Yes, I would — but not professionally, because I chose a different career path: becoming a prosecutor."

Reflection

Evaluation

Working on this project helped me better understand my creative process and my identity as a designer. From the beginning, I was drawn to the contrast between ballet and industrial aesthetics because they seemed completely opposite. Ballet was associated with softness, elegance, and fragility, while reflective surfaces represented structure, sharpness, and a futuristic aesthetic. Over time, the project became much more personal and emotional than I originally expected. I especially enjoyed working on this theme because after reading books and researching ballet, discipline, and femininity, I had wanted to create something like this for a long time. This project became an opportunity for me to combine visual inspiration, research, and my own ideas into one collection.

Visiting Hajime Sorayama's Sexy Robot exhibition gave me more confidence to try unexpected combinations and push my ideas further. Overall, this project helped me become more confident in my creative identity, showing me how to express emotion and meaning through fashion — not just visually, but through concept, material, and detail.
