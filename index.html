The Concept

Ballet meets Metal

This collection explores the contrast between ballet and metal — two opposite worlds united within a single image. A story about fragility and inner strength, inspired by the discipline, endurance, and resilience hidden behind the graceful appearance of a ballerina.

Metal symbolizes the inner core — strength, resilience, and emotional armor hidden beneath an outwardly soft appearance. Through the combination of soft silhouettes, flowing shapes, and metallic textures, I aimed to create a balance between tenderness and hardness, vulnerability and power.

Lina Nguen
Inspiration
"I came to ballet not through training, but through images — and found in it a language for everything I wanted to say through cloth."

The initial inspiration came from observing ballerinas and their ability to maintain beauty and poise despite pain, pressure, and constant self-discipline. Another important influence was the Sexy Robot exhibition by Hajime Sorayama in Shanghai, which introduced a futuristic and metallic aesthetic that inspired combining the vulnerability of the human body with the cold, rigid qualities of metal.

Interview

A Conversation with a Ballerina

01

What emotions did you feel when you first stepped onto the stage?

"At first, there was overwhelming nervousness, almost fear — but then it quickly turned into euphoria."

02

What was the most difficult thing about practicing ballet?

"Overcoming physical pain and mental pressure. Every practice had to be perfect, no mistakes allowed. But when you stand on stage, everything is worth it."

03

How did ballet change you as a person?

"It taught me discipline and perseverance. Even when your body hurts, you still have to smile and soar. That is quiet strength."

04

If you had the chance, would you return to ballet?

"Yes, I would — but not professionally, because I chose a different career path: becoming a prosecutor."

Reflection

Written Evaluation

Working on this project, I learned a lot about myself as a designer. Researching ballerinas and understanding how much discipline, pain, and physical strength exist behind their graceful appearance changed my perspective on femininity — softness does not equal weakness. This idea became the core of my collection.

Visiting Hajime Sorayama's Sexy Robot exhibition gave me more confidence to try unexpected combinations and push my ideas further. Overall, this project helped me become more confident in my creative identity, showing me how to express emotion and meaning through fashion — not just visually, but through concept, material, and detail.
