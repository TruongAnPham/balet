PROCESS

research. material. becoming.

“My engagement with ballet began not through technical dance training, but through images, narratives, and representations. Archival photographs of classical ballet, rehearsal documentation, and contemporary media portrayals revealed a recurring contrast between the polished image of the ballerina and the intense discipline that underlies it.”

“99 out of 100.”

Interview with former ballerina, Block 3

INTER VIEWS

“What age did you start studying ballet?”

“I was 14 when i first started”

“Was the beginning difficult for you? If so, how did you cope with the challenges?”

“The transition from one style of dance to another was not easy. I tried to give my all during rehearsals and prove to some of my teachers that I, too, was capable of much.”

“What emotions did you feel when you first stepped onto the stage?”

“At first, there was overwhelming nervousness, almost fear — but then it quickly turned into euphoria.”

“If you had the chance, would you return to ballet?”

“Yes, I would — but not professionally, because I chose a different career path: becoming a prosecutor.”

TIME LINE

BLOCK 1

“Emotional Entry”

A strong desire to work hard and prove oneself. Joy — the feeling of becoming part of the ballet world. Nervousness, excitement, trembling anticipation.

BLOCK 2

“The Struggles”

Insecurity — the need to prove worth to teachers. The pain of transition from one dance style to another.

BLOCK 3 — MAIN FOCUS

“The Dream”

The entry into a new world where joy intertwines with uncertainty. Ballet as emotional release — finding strength through discipline. Total dedication: 99 out of 100.
